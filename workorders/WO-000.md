Implementation notes

  What this memo is for. PLAN section 3 tabulates every environment parameter with an arm, a
  Phase-1 value and a sweep range. Six rows are marked provisional: their Phase-1 values are
  placeholders that gate G1 replaces with values the human picks from the interior of the DP regime
  map (PLAN sections 5, 13), recorded in `runs/G1_decision.md`. This memo is the evidence base for
  that pick, and the source of the `source` column of `gosplan/params.py` (WO-001). It is not a
  decision document: it does not choose Phase-1 values, and it does not rank arms.

  The eight research items of PLAN section 15, in full.

    Item 1 - Historical bonus schedules. Notch size relative to base pay, the per-percentage-point
    bonus increment above target, and any cap on accrual. Literature named in PLAN section 15:
    Berliner (1957), Nove, and the later reform literature. Feeds: `notch_height` (beta),
    `overfulfilment_slope` (s), `overfulfilment_cap` (rho_cap). Note that beta is the normalising
    unit of the reward scale (PLAN section 2.9.1), so what matters for the design is s and rho_cap
    RELATIVE to beta, not any of the three in absolute currency.

    Item 2 - Growth directives. Annual plan growth targets by period of Soviet history. Feeds:
    `growth_directive` (g), registry range [0, 0.07]. g is a treatment variable and the forcing
    term behind review finding F1, so the item must give a range across periods rather than a
    single national figure: at g = 0 with reports at target the target rule has a fixed point (test
    T-B2), which is precisely why g cannot be a constant.

    Item 3 - Ratchet. The theoretical treatment (Weitzman 1980) and any empirical estimate of how
    strongly next period's target responds to this period's fulfilment. Feeds: `ratchet_lambda`
    (lambda), registry range [0, 1]. Second question, kept separate: did tolerance bands exist -
    a band of fulfilment around 1 within which the target did not move? Feeds: `ratchet_deadband`
    (delta), range [0, 0.03], Phase-1 value 0.0 and inert. If the record supports the FORM but no
    value, say so and use the unsourced sentence.

    Item 4 - Inspection frequency and sanction structure for pripiski (padded reporting). Whatever
    can be said about how often enterprises were inspected, what the sanction was, and whether it
    scaled with the size of the discrepancy or was a fixed consequence of being caught at all.
    Feeds: `audit_rate` (a, range [0.01, 0.30]), `penalty_scale` (pen, range [5, 200]) and the two
    SHAPE parameters `penalty_form` (proportional vs fixed) and `penalty_arg` (positive_part vs
    absolute). The shape questions matter more than the level: `a * pen` is the compound quantity
    the G2 padding-elasticity criterion sweeps (PLAN section 4.5), and `penalty_arg` together with
    the holding loss h is the mechanism behind the held-out hidden-reserves phenomenon (PLAN
    section 4.2). Record separately whether inspection compared the claim to STOCK or to
    PRODUCTION: the environment compares it to stock on hand (PLAN section 2.8), and that choice
    deserves an evidential note here even if it remains a modelling assumption.

    Item 5 - Managerial tenure and rotation. How long an enterprise director typically held the
    post, and how rotation was decided. Feeds: `tenure` (psi), registry range [0.7, 0.98], the
    per-period continuation probability of the geometric horizon (PLAN section 2.12). psi is an
    ECONOMIC parameter and is deliberately distinct from the technical PPO discount gamma = 0.99;
    the memo must not conflate them, and must state the period length it converts from, since a
    per-year hazard is not a per-plan-period hazard unless the plan period is a year.

    Item 6 - Enterprise-level fulfilment distributions in archival work, for example Harrison's
    Gosplan-archive studies. Two outputs, both load-bearing. (i) The TARGET SHAPE for phenomenon 1
    of PLAN section 4.1: what a histogram of plan fulfilment actually looked like, so the bunching
    estimator has something to be compared against. (ii) The LEVEL at which the `forensic-stats`
    Soviet series is observed - enterprise, ministry or republic - because a series aggregated
    above the enterprise cannot speak to an enterprise-level notch, and that is what decides what
    PLAN section 7.3 may promise. State the level explicitly; if it is unknown, state that the
    coupling is unpromised until it is known.

    Item 7 - The Uzbek cotton affair. Magnitude, level and mechanism: was the padding done at the
    ministry or republic layer, or at the enterprise layer? Output: a judgement on whether this
    episode is admissible as an anchor for the Phase-2 ministry layer (PLAN section 2.14) AT ALL.
    "Not an anchor" is a perfectly good answer and is preferable to a stretched one.

    Item 8 - Tooling: current availability, versions and prices. Reference PPO implementations, in
    particular the one WO-017 will pin, with its version, since the reference-PPO version goes into
    every run manifest under CONTRACT rule 10; JAX MARL scaffolds for the WO-029 port; `rliable`
    for the interval estimates of PLAN section 4.3; open-source MIP solvers for the PLAN section
    6.2 oracle, with the solver version and optimality gap the manifest also requires. Also: the
    specific current models behind the `MID-fast` and `MID-strong` tiers and their prices, because
    PLAN section 12.1 requires the lead to record that tier-to-model mapping in the manifest at
    issue time and PLAN section 14 budgets against those prices. Date-stamp this item.

  Closing rule (PLAN section 15), which governs every item above. Each item ends with either a
  sourced range or the sentence "unsourced; prior = [...]; sensitivity handled by regime map /
  Sobol". NO ITEM MAY END WITH A POINT VALUE PRESENTED AS HISTORICAL FACT. A range with a citation
  is evidence. A range without one is a prior, and must be labelled a prior in those words. A point
  value with neither is the failure this card exists to prevent, because downstream it will be read
  as a fact about the Soviet economy rather than as a modelling choice, and no later gate undoes
  that.

  Presentation. Give each item its own section; within a section, separate (a) what the question
  is, (b) what was found, with citations, (c) what it implies for the named parameters, as a range,
  and (d) the closing line the rule above requires. Where a source gives a figure that cannot be
  converted into the model's units without an assumption, state the assumption on its own line
  rather than folding it into the number.
