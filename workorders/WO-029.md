## Must pass (verbatim test ids)

**None are frozen yet beyond the parity criterion below** — the revision writes the test file and
names its id here (CONTRACT rule 12).

**The one acceptance criterion PLAN already states exactly** (PLAN §12.4, and the G3 row of PLAN
§13, "JAX parity"):

> **100 agent-steps driven by `TruthfulMyopic` are identical to the NumPy implementation to
> `1e-5`, under the same key-based RNG.**

It is a meaningful test rather than a tautology only because of PLAN §2.15: every draw is keyed as
`SeedSequence([seed_env, crc32(purpose), *indices])` and is therefore **order-independent**, so the
two implementations agree by construction rather than by both walking one stream in the same order.
A port that reproduced the trajectory only by preserving call order would be one refactor away from
silently diverging.

Also required by PLAN §12.4: the **capital**, **IRS** and **setup-cost** branches are ported under
`lax.cond` / `jnp.where` and each gets its own parity case — they are Phase-2 toggles, off in Phase
1, and therefore the easiest to get wrong without noticing.

Green after this change: the full frozen Phase-1 suite on the NumPy path, unchanged.

## Implementation notes

Provisional until the lead revises the spec.

**Branching.** `where` where both sides are cheap and defined; `lax.cond` where evaluating the
untaken side would be wrong or wasteful. The discontinuities the design deliberately contains and
the port must not round off:

```
Λ_w(x) = 1[x ≥ 0] at w = 0, and the ρ_cap = ∞ branch of bonus      (§2.8)
the θ = ∞ (min) branch of the coverage aggregator                  (§2.6)
the claimed_i == 0 convention fill_i = 1 in deliver                (§2.7.3)
the σ_c → 1 Cobb–Douglas limit of the welfare index                (§2.9.3)
the setup cost F · 1[e > 0], a genuine discontinuity at e = 0      (§2.6)
```

A port that rounds a Heaviside into a sigmoid has changed the economics, not the backend:
`notch_width` is a treatment variable and the smooth arm is a configuration.

**Contract, unchanged by the backend.** `Array` is the alias every signature is written against and
the port substitutes its own type behind that name, so no module may rely on a numpy-only method in
a signature. CONTRACT rule 9 governs randomness identically (no global generator, no stream
threading, no `jax.random` key split standing in for a keyed draw). Rule 4 forbids a vectorised
rollout any per-step shaping and any running reward normalisation. Rule 6 keeps whatever the port
emits for the ledger off every agent-facing path. Rule 8 keeps the `at_bound` accounting intact so
`BOUND_BINDING` still fires.

What the revision must settle before the port is written: the final Phase-2 dynamics themselves — in
particular the trade matcher of WO-024, whose greedy loop is the one piece of PLAN §§2.5–2.14 with
no obvious vectorised form, and whose tie-breaking must be identical on both backends for the parity
test to mean anything.

## Forbidden

- Delegating this card. PLAN §1.3 finding F14 keeps it with the LEAD.
- Changing `gosplan/env/` to make the port easier. The NumPy path is the reference for the golden
  files (T-B7); if a change to it is genuinely needed, it is a separate LEAD change with a
  `spec/CHANGELOG.md` entry and regenerated goldens (rules 1, 2).
- Smoothing any of the discontinuities listed above, "for gradient stability" or otherwise.
- Any `jax.random` call inside the ported environment (rule 9).
- Reporting a parity failure as a tolerance question. 1e-5 over 100 agent-steps is the criterion; a
  port that needs a looser tolerance has a bug, and the bug is the finding.
- Computing PLAN §4.1 rows **2, 5, 6, 7** — held out until WO-030.

## Compute budget (PLAN §14)

The port is what makes "P2 acceptance | ≈8 configs × 30 seeds | ≈1–2 days CPU or **hours JAX**" and
the Sobol row ("JAX only") affordable. PLAN §14 also records why Phase 1 was deliberately
NumPy-only: so the port is not on the critical path to the first real check.
